# Product hypothesis and adjacent solutions

Crisis2Action tests a narrow hypothesis: a municipal coordinator may benefit from a traceable chain from a hydrological signal to a reviewable institutional briefing. We have demonstrated the software chain; we have not validated customer demand, market size, willingness to pay, time savings or damage reduction.

## Demonstrated distinction

- Real local historical discharge is kept separate from synthetic what-if values.
- Demo classifications have a versioned threshold explanation and cannot create legal powers.
- Institutional cards retain source, version limits, translation state, conditions and unverified fields.
- The knowledge-package matrix exposes missing mappings instead of alleging legislative gaps.
- An acknowledgement is bound to exact data/rule/legal/fact contents and is required before producing an unapproved draft.
- Rule and legal-package swaps change results with no source-code changes.

These are demonstrated properties of this prototype, **not claims that competing products lack them**.

## Value and business experiments

First user: a generic municipal crisis coordinator; territorial assignment remains pending. Potential buyer: municipality, utility, consultancy or platform integrator. Plausible model: a configured pilot, then maintenance/integration per organization. No price, market size or revenue is validated.

Pilot tasks: use the existing workflow and Crisis2Action on the same historical case; measure time to produce a correct source-linked brief, missing prerequisite detection, unsupported-claim rate, reviewer corrections and reuse. A faster incorrect brief is not success. Do not claim prevented floods, lives saved or percentages of water savings.
